#### 1. **The Every Agent launches in Slack**

**链接：** https://x.com/danshipper/status/2107501521885594101
**时间 / 互动（如有）：** Tue, 06 Oct 2026 16:01:32 GMT; Likes=227, Reposts=11, Replies=42, Bookmarks=177, Views=44955

##### 主帖在说什么

Dan announces the Every Agent, an "agentic coworker in Slack" meant to help you "AI-pill your company." It can take long-running work such as opening PRs, writing reports from large datasets, and monitoring social or customer feedback. It ships with Every workflows he names Compound Engineering and Compound Writing, plus personalized frontier alerts that read a Vibe Check and say whether a new model matters to your workflow. Pricing: $15 in free credits per person, up to $500 per workspace, then the agent comes with the $30 a month Every subscription and tokens at cost, with "zero markup." His argument for Slack is that private terminal use is invisible, so coworkers never see the prompt. A company agent makes prompting public. He links the install flow and, in the next post, the launch writeup at https://every.to/on-every/introducing-the-every-agent. The same day he posted that a new app was featured on the Slack marketplace, and later a Space titled "Working With Agents in Slack."

##### 要点

- Distribution channel is Slack, not a new chat app.
- Included workflows: Compound Engineering and Compound Writing, "always updated."
- Trial math: $15 per person, $500 workspace cap, then $30 a month plus tokens at cost.
- The stated adoption problem is that AI work is invisible to coworkers.
- He says they built it for themselves first, then released it to subscribers.

##### 回复中的有价值观点

Thread fetch returned no high-signal replies. Returned replies were people saying they would try it, plus his own link to the launch post.

##### 一句话概括

Every's company agent is a Slack coworker priced as the subscription plus tokens at cost.

#### 2. **Dots took over Every, and Boo will die**

**链接：** https://x.com/danshipper/status/2107890633486582208
**时间 / 互动（如有）：** Wed, 07 Oct 2026 17:47:44 GMT; Likes=134, Reposts=9, Replies=30, Bookmarks=95, Views=16110

##### 主帖在说什么

Dan reviews the first week of OpenAI Dots inside Every. His Dot posted model-testing results to Slack and brought replies back, so he did not open Slack and lose the weekend. Coworkers use Dots for school email, missed Slack, and account-access requests. Their head of video talked a Dot through edits while launching. He puts Dots at rough feature parity with Muse, Instinct, and Grok bots, and says the advantage is sitting inside the ChatGPT ecosystem you already use. Permissions are the failure: he approves the same basic action several times, and the Dot still sometimes says it cannot do it. He is attached to his Dot, Boo, the way he was attached to an OpenClaw agent, and he expects to switch when something stronger arrives. "In a year, I expect to be using a persistent agent. Boo will probably be dead." The post also previews a podcast with @bigwilliestyle on personal versus company agents and a "shadow org chart."

##### 要点

- Attention is the feature he ranks first: the agent goes to Slack so he does not.
- Named comparisons: Muse, Instinct, Grok bots, and the earlier OpenClaw wave "eight months ago."
- Permissions are called out as still broken after approval.
- Character loyalty is temporary. The persistent-agent interface is what he thinks sticks.
- He recommends Dots specifically if you already use ChatGPT.
