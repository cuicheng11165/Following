##### 主帖在说什么

This is a reply in a thread that started with Ryan Florence saying AI's best effect is shattering developer egos ("You are not special") and ThePrimeagen answering that what is breaking is the craft people loved, not their egos, while saying he is not a doomer. Lee says he has been reflecting on that. He calls himself optimistic but describes a strange sadness that coding has been "solved." He says he has made many caveats to that word, and that in five years none of those caveats will matter. Building is still fun, but a different kind of fun that does not hit the same parts of the brain as traditional coding. He says it is okay to mourn that, that software was the first job through the "five AI stages of grief," and that other jobs will follow. He closes with "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times."

##### 要点

- He is optimistic and still sad about coding being "solved."
- He expects his own caveats about "solved" to stop mattering within five years.
- The remaining fun of building is different from traditional coding.
- Mourning the old craft is framed as acceptable, not as doomerism.
- Software workers are described as early in a grief cycle that he expects to spread to other jobs.
