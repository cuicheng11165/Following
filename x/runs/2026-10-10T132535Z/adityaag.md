#### 1. **Three paths for an ambitious engineer**

**链接：** https://x.com/adityaag/status/2107865115831988530
**时间 / 互动（如有）：** Wed, 07 Oct 2026 16:06:20 GMT; Likes=221, Reposts=16, Replies=23, Bookmarks=216, Views=17103

##### 主帖在说什么

Aditya asks what an ambitious software engineer should do now. Ten years ago, being good at software was a rare skill you could point at payments, hospitals, or communication, and "get really good at software engineering" was enough advice because you could pick the industry later. AI makes working software widely available, so "I can build it" is a weaker personal advantage even though great engineering is still rare. His three paths: work on whatever is eating software (research, infrastructure, tools) where technical ability is still the constraint; team up with someone who has spent about fifteen years in a domain and knows the expensive mistakes; or supervise coding agents doing the same backlog, which he finds least compelling. At SPC he says more promising founders now show up without much software background. He would still get very good at engineering, then put it next to a frontier or a problem, and use the extra building capacity to take responsibility for something bigger.

##### 要点

- The old advice was skill first, application later. He says that order is weaker now.
- Path 1 is the AI stack itself, not a random app.
- Path 2 is a domain expert plus an engineer, and he says SPC is already seeing the non-engineer founder.
- Path 3, agent-shepherding the same tickets, is the one he tells ambitious people to skip.
- Bookmarks are roughly equal to likes.

#### 2. **The best doctor still has half an hour**

##### 主帖在说什么

Aditya says it is a misconception that being very rich buys unlimited time with the world's best doctor, lawyer, or creative professional. The leading cancer doctor still sees you for 20–30 minutes no matter how much money you have. AI is "crazy" because it both democratizes access and makes time unlimited for every individual. He calls that the practical effect of intelligence too cheap to meter. This is his highest-like original essay in the window.

##### 要点

- The scarce input is expert minutes, not a software seat.
- The number he uses is 20–30 minutes, even for cancer care.
- The AI claim is access plus unlimited time, not a specific medical product.
- He does not discuss accuracy, liability, or regulation in the post.

#### 5. **Only the maximalist version gets funded**

##### 主帖在说什么

The question Aditya says he keeps asking at SPC is "What is the maximalist version of this company?" He thinks that if you are building now, you need the boldest version of the idea and of how it changes the future. Starting small, he says, makes it harder to get funding, hire, and make a dent. He calls this the paradox of AI: building is easier, and the floor for a great startup is much higher.

##### 要点

- The advice is explicitly about fundraising and hiring, not just ambition as a mood.
- "Start small" is the failure mode he names.
- He allows that the maximalist bar may always have been true, and says AI makes it sharper.
- The post includes a photo and no example company.
