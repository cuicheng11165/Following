#### 1. **Tab-complete the agent, not just the code**

##### 主帖在说什么

Nan is reacting to Tibo's "Day 5" note that composer predictions in the Codex desktop app are often accurate enough to cause a double take, and that they are included in Pro plans without consuming usage. He says he cannot stress how much he has loved the feature, and he places it in a four-step history of programming interfaces: writing code by hand, tab-completing code, prompting agents, and now tab-completing that prompt too. In the next post in the thread he asks whether this is recursive self-improvement. https://x.com/thenanyu/status/2108671887266246730

##### 要点

- The feature he is praising is composer predictions in the desktop app, on Pro, without consuming usage (Tibo's claim, which he is endorsing).
- His own claim is experiential: he has loved using it.
- The historical frame is hand-written code, then tab-complete, then prompted agents, then tab-complete on the agent prompt.
- He floats "recursive self-improvement" as a joke-question, not a technical claim.

#### 4. **Unban the agents; it is a config file**

##### 主帖在说什么

Paul Graham argued that Amazon banning agents is the first real opening, since Amazon was founded, for a startup to compete with Amazon, because people will want their own agents to buy things and will not want Amazon's agent. Nan's reply is that Amazon can simply unban agents, and that this is a single config file. In the same conversation he adds that Amazon will make the money back serving inference for those bots, and that "They have AWS they can handle it." https://x.com/thenanyu/status/2108057005198037494 https://x.com/thenanyu/status/2108093142415331488

##### 要点

- He rejects the idea that an agent ban is a durable opening for a competitor.
- The mechanism he names is a config change, not a product rewrite.
- His monetization guess is inference revenue, via AWS, rather than a new retail surface.
- He does not dispute that people will want agents to shop.

#### 5. **Unloved systems will degrade**

**链接：** https://x.com/thenanyu/status/2107506074370920796
**时间 / 互动（如有）：** Tue, 06 Oct 2026 16:19:38 GMT; Likes=32 Replies=2 Views=5867

##### 主帖在说什么

DHH wrote that he sympathizes with programmers who only enjoyed the mechanical act of turning tickets into code for systems and customers they did not care about, and that you have to care about the software to be excited about making it with agents. Nan does not argue the joy point. He adds a maintenance worry: if care is the precondition for agent-assisted work, systems that nobody loves, but that someone still has to keep alive, will degrade badly.

##### 要点

- He accepts DHH's premise that agent enthusiasm tracks care.
- The risk he names is neglect of necessary but unloved systems, not a loss of craft in the systems people do love.
- He does not propose a process or staffing fix.
- "You can just fix things" is a separate in-window line, not a solution attached to this post. https://x.com/thenanyu/status/2107262460675891640
